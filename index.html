01PM Intern

PM Intern · Instagram

Q4 2024 Product Features Overview

Creators wanted better short-form tools, small accounts could not get discovered, and shopping worked differently everywhere. I picked four features, sized each against real behavioural evidence, and wrote the KPI and risk plan behind them.

Open dossier +Close dossier –

The Problem

From user personas and reviews: users seek more creative tools for short-form content; limited discoverability for smaller creators; inconsistent shopping experiences across platforms.

Research

Two personas carried the work: Influencer Ingrid, 27, maximizing reach and monetization; Casual Casey, 22, sharing moments creatively. Competitor read: TikTok dominates short-form video but lacks robust e-commerce integration; Snapchat excels in AR but lags in creator monetization. 71% of Gen Z prefer short-form video.

The Call I Made

Ship four features, each tied to a number I could defend: Enhanced Reels Editing Tools (90% watch Reels weekly; 65% of Reels views come from non-followers), AI-Powered Content Suggestions (87% say relevance affects time on platform; AI-recommended posts went from 15% of feed in 2023 to 50%), Collaborative Stories (500M daily Story users; projected +25% Story engagement), Integrated Shopping for Reels (44% shop weekly in-app; shoppable Reels convert 20% higher than static posts).

Risks I Planned For

User confusion with new tools → step-by-step tutorials and influencer demonstrations. Competitor copies the features → accelerate rollout, emphasize the integrations only Instagram has. Technical glitches at launch → comprehensive testing with phased beta releases.

Before → After

Before: a feature wish-list with no shared success criteria. After: three named KPIs with owners — 12% average engagement rate on promoted posts (marketing), 20% adoption of the new Reels editing tools among active creators inside 60 days (product), $50M in shoppable Reels sales in Q4 (revenue) — on a Sep kickoff → Oct demo day → Nov campaign → Dec launch timeline.

What I Would Do Differently

I leaned on secondary research and two personas. Next time I would run five moderated sessions with small creators specifically, because discoverability was the pain point I had the least first-hand evidence for — and the one my roadmap treated as solved.

Product Strategy Intern · Miracle Mile Advisors

User-Research-Driven Feature Roadmap

Wealth clients could not find anything in the advisory dashboard. I mined interview data from 150+ users inside Salesforce, built two personas at opposite ends of tech proficiency, and turned the findings into a four-month roadmap the consulting team could actually staff.

Open dossier +Close dossier –

The Problem

Top pain points from research: difficulty navigating existing financial dashboards, and limited personalization in product offerings. Users were asking for AI-driven portfolio recommendations and a genuinely better UI.

Research

Planning → data collection → analysis → reporting, across 150+ user interviews held in Salesforce. Most-identified needs: a simplified dashboard, real-time alerts, AI-driven recommendations, mobile optimization. Two personas framed the extremes — Mary Lee, 34, financial analyst, high tech proficiency: “Interface has too many options.” Edward Johnson, 62, retired, moderate proficiency: “dashboard feels too overwhelming; I just need a snapshot of my portfolio.”

The Tradeoff

On an impact-versus-effort matrix I deliberately chose high-impact, high-effort work rather than the easy wins: the four-month timeline gave us real access to the tech team, and consultants had client load that made small iterative asks expensive to coordinate. Short term, quick wins on AI-driven client dashboards. Long term, proprietary financial planning software for current and future clients.

Cross-Functional

Audited the three features already live — advisor messaging, quarterly financial summaries, goal-based scenario modeling — then benchmarked Miracle Mile against Hightower, Telemus, Invesco and Lucia on fee transparency, technology use, tailored services, product portfolio and client satisfaction. Roadmap ran April testing → May user feedback → June mid-point check-in with the tech teams → July launch and second feedback round.

Before → After

Before: a dashboard built outward from what the firm could report. After: three prioritized recommendations — intuitive navigation, a customizable dashboard, AI integration — with success measured on CSAT and NPS, increase in assets under management, and market share growth in target demographics. Q1–Q2 execute quick wins and pilot the new tools, Q3–Q4 evaluate against client feedback, year two scale what worked.

What I Would Do Differently

The interview data already existed in Salesforce, so my analysis inherited whatever the sales conversations happened to ask. I would add a short structured survey of my own to test the two personas against each other, instead of trusting that Mary and Edward covered the middle of the base.

The Problem

Pain points identified in research: complex onboarding for new users, lack of integrated social features, limited support for diverse content formats. Founded in 2023 by Joshua Blackwell, the platform gives creators — independent artists through large brands — the tools to design, produce and monetize immersive experiences at $29.99/month with a seven-day trial.

Research

Primary audience millennials 25–40 and Gen Z 18–24; secondary early tech adopters and creative professionals. 85% of users prioritize seamless interaction in immersive environments, 70% prefer customizable experiences, and users named “intuitive navigation” and “high-quality visuals” as their top engagement priorities.

Prioritization

Epics scored with RICE and sized in story points before anything entered a sprint — the performance epic came in at RICE 700 and 21 points, the integration epics at 575 and 13. Four features made the cut: Multi-Sensory Integration, Dynamic Content Library, Intuitive User Interface, Collaborative Virtual Spaces, each with its own testing and rollout steps.

Cross-Functional

One timeline across four teams — Design, Data, Strategy, Product — covering collaboration 1.0, user permissions, live collaboration, A/B testing and feature flags, load-time optimization, KPI dashboards, event tracking and monitoring. Growth was modelled B2C2B across acquisition, activation and retention, revenue and referral, with the metric set named up front: installs, site visits, CTR, view count, social shares, email and LinkedIn open rates, session frequency, NPS, churn, referred customers, viral reels, app ratings.

Before → After

Before: a feature list and no sequencing. After: a milestone journey the whole company could read — Q1 2024 launch enhanced onboarding, Q3 2024 roll out Creator Studio for beta testing, Q1 2025 release cross-platform AR/VR hybrid features, Q3 2025 full integration with next-gen wearables, Q1 2026 explore and solidify three further integration methods. User feedback on the prototypes came back “so convenient”, “I love this!”, “visually appealing”.

What I Would Do Differently

The 2026 horizon was further out than the evidence justified — wearable integration was a bet, not a finding. I would have cut the roadmap at Creator Studio and held the back half as an explicitly labelled hypothesis, so the team could not mistake ambition for commitment.

Project Manager · Chanel × Jennie Kim

Coco Neige 2021–2022 Campaign Analysis

Does ambassador fit actually move product, or just impressions? I ran an in-depth analysis of the campaign and the ambassador behind it, and separated the metrics that signalled attention from the ones that signalled purchase.

The Question

Chanel had made Jennie Kim the face of Coco Neige. The interesting question was not whether the campaign was popular — it was whether the ambassador choice was defensible on revenue.

Research

Ambassador profile: member of BLACKPINK, 86M+ Instagram followers, 6.8M on YouTube, approximately 1.5M tickets across 66 shows on the BORN PINK world tour, which grossed over $260 million; face of Calvin Klein's Spring 2021 collection; known as “Human Chanel”. Campaign themes were Outdoor Adventure, Global Appeal and Winter Elegance, running across news and billboards, social posts and exclusive video.

Results I Traced

2021 Chanel × Jennie: +30% domestic sales, +66% operating profit, roughly 1.6 trillion KRW. 2022–2023: +72% domestic sales, +55% operating profit, roughly 4.3 trillion KRW. Campaign posts on @chanel drew 30M+ likes; her own posts featuring Chanel drew 75M+. #JennieForChanel trended in 20+ countries and Chanel's Instagram following grew by 1.2M. Billboard visuals in Paris, Seoul and New York were picked up by 35+ news outlets.

What The Data Said

Behind-the-scenes video consistently outperformed static posts. One clip of Jennie talking about her love of winter sports took 30M+ views and 45k+ shares in three days, and post-campaign surveys had 67% citing her storytelling as a reason they felt more connected to Chanel. Attention followed the personal footage, not the produced imagery.

Before → After

Before: ambassador fit argued on cultural relevance and follower count. After: a fit argument that runs from audience overlap to sell-through, with the recommendation to weight budget toward unpolished behind-the-scenes video because that is where engagement and stated emotional connection both concentrated.

What I Would Do Differently

The sales lift is correlation on a campaign with several things happening at once. I would state the confound explicitly and look for a comparable collection launched without an ambassador in the same window, rather than letting the +40% carry more weight than it can bear.

Product Manager · Red Bull Juneberry

Summer Edition — Launch Strategy & Roadmap

A seasonal flavour launch in a category where everyone launches seasonal flavours. I built the market case, the competitor read and the go-to-market roadmap for a summer edition that had to earn shelf space against Red Bull's own core line.

$91B

7.9%

45%+
Of the global market sits in North America
3

Open dossier +Close dossier –

The Problem

Seasonal editions are cheap to announce and expensive to get wrong: they cannibalise the core line if the flavour reads as a substitute rather than an occasion. Juneberry needed a reason to exist that a distributor would accept.

Market Analysis

North America accounts for over 45% of the global market, driven by lifestyle trends. Asia-Pacific is the fastest-growing region, with urbanisation and rising awareness of energy-boosting products. Europe shows stable growth focused on health-conscious formulations and natural ingredients. Summer trends: high demand for refreshing flavours, and a consumer priority on portability and convenience.

The Call I Made

Position Juneberry as an occasion, not a flavour: vibrant taste with notes of juneberry, cherry and red grape, sold on refreshment and portability rather than on higher performance. That framing kept it out of competition with the core can and made seasonal retail partnerships the primary distribution play.

Competitive Set

Mapped the field before writing the plan — who competes on price, who on performance, who on flavour novelty — so the launch narrative could claim the one position nobody in the summer set was holding.

Before → After

Before: a flavour idea with a season attached. After: a launch with a market size, a regional strategy for each of the three major regions, a competitor position, and seasonal sales partnerships as the distribution mechanism.

What I Would Do Differently

I built the case on secondary market data. A single taste test with fifty people in the target region would have told me more about whether juneberry reads as refreshing than any CAGR chart did.

Product Overview · Shiseido Future Solution LX

Total Regenerating Cream — Launched Nov 2024

A teardown of a real luxury launch, end to end: the market Shiseido was launching into while its own revenue was falling, the R&D evidence the claims rested on, and which marketing tactics actually moved the brand's trust score.

Open dossier +Close dossier –

The Context

Global skincare was valued at roughly $109.71 billion in 2023 and is projected to grow from $115.65 billion to $194.05 billion by 2032. Asia-Pacific led with a 51.49% share. Shiseido holds 7.6% globally and 9.3% of Japan's beauty and personal care market, with skincare making up 75% of its sales — while company revenue was down 8.83% from its 2019 peak. Launching a flagship into that gap is the interesting decision.

The Product

The LonGenevity Complex, an anti-aging formula targeting wrinkles, dullness and loss of firmness, in a rich overnight texture. Claims backed by clinical work: fine lines and wrinkles reduced 25% in four weeks, hydration improved 40% overnight, 92% of users reporting visible firmness and elasticity gains after eight weeks, 98% overall satisfaction. Packaging in 30% recycled materials, airtight to preserve the actives, drawn from traditional Japanese urushi lacquerware.

What Actually Worked

Four tactics observed, and the evidence for each: emphasis on video content produced a 79% year-over-year increase in video views. Experiential events plus VIP influencer partnerships lifted the Visibility, Impact and Trust score 54% — the 2021 Malibu Beach House event alone generated 154 million impressions and 348,000 engagements. The #POWERFULEYE campaign distributed samples through an Instagram story-activated chatbot and cleared an 80%+ in-store redemption rate.

The Read

Sampling with a redemption mechanic was the highest-conversion tactic in the set and the cheapest to instrument. Prestige events bought brand trust; the chatbot bought trial. A launch plan should not have to choose, but if it does, trial wins.

Before → After

Before: a launch narrative told through heritage and ingredients. After: the same launch read as a system — market position, R&D proof, packaging decisions and four measurable marketing tactics, each with the number that justifies keeping it.

What I Would Do Differently

I treated the reported figures as given. For a launch I owned, I would want the sampling redemption and the VIT lift measured against a holdout region, because the numbers currently prove the tactics ran, not that they caused the result.
